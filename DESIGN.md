# Design System: NEXA

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Direct Line"**

NEXA is a warm, quiet room with one wire running through it. The room is stone: near-black neutrals with a trace of warmth, separated by hairline seams rather than by shadow. The wire is the one precious thing in it, a thin line in the accent colour that leaves the composer and ends at the exact host a message will reach. Everything else stays calm so that this line can be read, because the product's promise is that nothing sits between the user and their provider.

The system is built for hours of use, so density is that of a working tool, not a showcase: 14px controls, 15px interface text, 16px reading text, and one colour held back for what matters. The landing page lives in the same world at a louder volume. It uses the same stone, the same accent and the same wire, with Funnel Display at scale and the wire drawn as the spine of the page.

The system refuses three things by name: blue as a base colour (rejected by the founder), the grey vendor chat clone, and the glowing AI gradient. Dark is the default, Light and System are full peers, and five user-selectable palettes change the material without changing any rule here.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Warm stone neutrals derived from one hue and one tint, so every palette reads as a single material.
- One accent, reserved for the primary action, focus, selection, live state and the wire.
- Hairline seams at rest; shadow only on things that truly float.
- The wire: a literal, truthful route line naming the provider host.
- Machine strings in JetBrains Mono, numbers in tabular figures.
- Phones are a first-class layout: drawer, bottom sheets, 44px targets.

## Colors

One precious colour on stone. Frontmatter values show the default Gold palette; the first group is the dark theme and the keys ending in `-light` are the light theme. OKLCH is canonical, and `src/styles/tokens.css` is the source of truth.

### Primary
- **Worked Gold** (`accent`): primary buttons, the send button, focus rings, text selection, input carets, active field and composer borders, the selected check, the stream cursor, prose links, and the wire. On the landing page it also marks the logo and the last line of the tagline.
- **Burnished Gold** (`accent-light`): the same roles on light backgrounds, darkened for contrast (5.9:1 on the light background).
- **Gold Wash** (`accent-soft`): the selected conversation row and active icon buttons. A 15% mix, so selection is felt rather than announced.

### Neutral
- **Quarry Black** (`background-deep`): the sidebar, input wells and code blocks. The deepest step, used for recessed places.
- **Warm Stone** (`background`): the page and conversation plane.
- **Cut Stone** (`surface`, `surface-raised`, `surface-hover`): user messages and the composer, then dialogs and popovers, then hover fills. Each step is a small lift in lightness.
- **Limestone** (`foreground`): primary text.
- **Weathered** (`muted`): secondary text and resting icons (about 9:1 on the background).
- **Dust** (`faint`): captions, placeholders, line numbers and group labels (about 6:1 on the background, and above 5:1 on every resting surface in all five palettes).
- **Seam** (`border`, `border-strong`): hairlines between regions, and the firmer line for secondary buttons, the landing rail and hover states on fields.

### Status
- **Ember** (`danger`, `danger-soft`): destructive actions, invalid fields and error cards.
- **Verdigris** (`success`): the connected dot and the copied check.
- **Amber** (`warning`): the relay on the wire and favourite stars. It leans orange so it stays distinct from the Gold accent, and a warning still never relies on colour alone.

### Palettes
Five palettes exist, selected with `data-palette` on the root. A palette sets a hue, a tint strength and an accent in each theme. All neutrals are derived from the hue and tint. Surfaces and seams stay close to the base tint, and secondary text multiplies it (`muted` and `faint` by 1.6 in dark, by 2 in light).

- **Gold** (default): hue 75, tint 0.006.
- **Emerald:** hue 165, tint 0.012.
- **Violet:** hue 295, tint 0.016.
- **Coral:** hue 25, tint 0.01.
- **Mono:** hue 0, tint 0, with a near-white accent in dark and a near-black accent in light.

Browser chrome cannot read CSS variables, so `src/lib/palettes.ts` carries each palette's swatch and background as hex. These match the computed OKLCH values and must stay in step with them.

### Syntax
Six syntax colours (`syntax-*`) tint code in both themes. They are low in chroma, live only inside code, and do not change with the palette, apart from comments, which follow the palette hue.

### Named Rules
**The One Material Rule.** A palette is a hue, a tint and an accent. Never paste a new accent on existing neutrals; derive the neutrals from the same hue.

**The No Blue Rule.** Blue is never a base colour: no palette hue, neutral tint or accent may be blue. The founder rejected it, and the shipped palette hues leave everything between the teal of the syntax colours (190) and violet (295) empty. Syntax colours are not base colours.

**The Precious Colour Rule.** The accent means act here, you are here, or this is live. It is never decoration, and never a fill for a large region.

## Typography

**Display Font:** Funnel Display Variable (with Hanken Grotesk Variable, then system sans)
**Body Font:** Hanken Grotesk Variable (with system sans)
**Label/Mono Font:** JetBrains Mono Variable (with ui-monospace, SF Mono, Cascadia Code, Menlo, Consolas)

**Character:** Funnel Display brings a slightly engineered warmth to headings, Hanken Grotesk stays out of the way for long sessions, and JetBrains Mono marks anything a machine would read. All three are self-hosted variable fonts.

### Hierarchy
- **Display** (600, fluid 48 to 76px, line-height 1, tight tracking): the landing hero only.
- **Closing headline** (600, fluid 36 to 64px): the final landing statement.
- **Headline** (600, fluid 28 to 40px): landing section titles.
- **Title** (600, 24px): empty states. **Small title** (600, 18px): dialog titles.
- **Prose** (400, 16px, line-height 1.68): rendered model answers. Prose headings use the display face, from 24px down to 16px, at weight 600.
- **Body** (400, 15px, line-height 1.5): the interface default, and text typed in the composer.
- **Control** (500, 14px): buttons, inputs, conversation titles. **Label** (500, 13px): field labels, small buttons, secondary lines.
- **Caption** (400, 12px): metadata, example notices.
- **Large title** (600, 44px, display face, 30px on phones): the empty chat's question and the first-run screens.
- **Micro** (400, 11px): the host in a message's usage line, and the profile's second line. **Overline** (500, 10.5px, uppercase, 0.08em): the sidebar's group labels and shortcut badges, from the founder's chosen sidebar, and nowhere else.
- **Mono** (400, 13px, line-height 1.6): code. Hosts, URLs and keys use the same face at 12 to 14px.
- **Wordmark** (600, 17px, wide 0.08em tracking): NEXA, and nothing else.

All sizes are in rem and follow `--text-scale` (0.9375, 1 or 1.0938), which the user sets in settings.

### Named Rules
**The Exact String Rule.** Hosts, URLs, keys, model identifiers and code are set in mono with ligatures off, because a ligature can make `://` misleading. Human language is never set in mono.

**The Tabular Rule.** Counts, token totals, costs, file sizes and line numbers use tabular figures.

## Layout

The app is a two-pane shell: a 280px sidebar and a conversation column centred at 760px, with the composer floating at the foot of that column and the wire beneath it. The shell fills the dynamic viewport and respects safe-area insets on every edge.

Breakpoints are Tailwind's defaults: 640, 768 and 1024px. From 768 to 1023px the sidebar narrows to 224px so the conversation keeps its width. Below 768px the sidebar becomes a drag-to-dismiss drawer of up to 85vw (320px at most), popovers and menus become bottom sheets, and dialogs dock to the bottom edge below 640px.

Spacing runs on a 4px base with half steps at 6, 10 and 14px for tight controls. 8 and 12px carry most gaps; 20 and 32px are the page gutters at phone and desktop width.

The landing page sits in a 1152px container. Its hero is a 5:6 split, the tagline on the left and the real interface on the right, with the wire running full width under both. Sections below hang off a single vertical rail as stations, each with a 5:7 split of statement and working demo. Reading measures are capped at 34 to 42ch.

**The Thumb Rule.** Below 768px every control reaches 44px, and fields use 16px text so iOS does not zoom. Hover styles are gated behind `(hover: hover)` so a tap never leaves a stuck state.

## Elevation & Depth

Depth is tonal first. Regions separate by small steps of lightness and 1px seams; the sidebar recedes to the deepest step and raised panels lift by a few points of lightness. There is one shadow, and it means the element is floating above the page plane.

### Shadow Vocabulary
- **Float** (`--shadow-float`, a soft 40px ambient layer with a tight 8px contact layer, lighter in the light theme): the composer, dialogs, floating panels and the landing product preview.

Scrims use `overlay` behind dialogs, drawers and sheets. No backdrop blur is used.

### Named Rules
**The Seam Rule.** Things in the page plane are separated by a hairline, never a shadow. Shadow is for things that float.

## Shapes

Corners are gently curved and stepped by size: 6px (`s`) for small controls and list rows, 10px (`m`) for buttons, fields, cards, code blocks and popovers, 16px (`l`) for the composer, dialogs and bottom sheets. Inner elements step down so nested corners stay concentric. Full rounds are kept for the wire's terminals, status dots, switches and sheet handles.

Lines are always 1px and straight. The logo is three butt-capped strokes, two terminals joined by one line, read together as an N. Its geometry is the same idea as the wire and is not to be softened.

All three radii apply to all four corners. In code they are `rounded-sm`, `rounded-md` and `rounded-lg`. Never name a radius token `s` or `l` in the Tailwind theme: `rounded-s` and `rounded-l` are Tailwind's start-side and left-side utilities.

## Components

Components are calm and exact. They respond in 100 to 150ms, press to 0.97 scale, and do not bounce. The composer is the one exception: the founder chose its spring, so its opening and its attachment tab overshoot slightly. Nothing else does.

### Buttons
Buttons share the sidebar's vocabulary, so the shell reads as one piece.
- **Shape:** 10px corners at every size; the large size uses 12px.
- **Sizes:** 32, 36 and 44px. They grow to 44px on phones and on any touch screen, tablets included (the `touch:` variant).
- **Primary:** the only coloured button. Accent fill, on-accent text, weight 600, and a faint top highlight.
- **Hover / Focus:** a slight shift toward white in dark (toward black in light), and a 2px accent outline at 2px offset on keyboard focus.
- **Secondary:** the default. A 4% foreground wash with a 10% foreground line, both firming on hover. No hard outline.
- **Ghost:** muted text, with a 6% foreground wash on hover.
- **Danger:** danger text with a 35% danger line, and no fill until hover or keyboard focus.
- **Disabled:** 40% opacity with a not-allowed cursor. A disabled primary loses its colour, so it never passes for an available action.

### Cards / Containers
- **Corner Style:** 10px.
- **Background:** usually transparent, on a `border` hairline. Lists use dividers inside a single outline, never a card per row.
- **Shadow Strategy:** none, by the Seam Rule.
- **Notice:** one card for toasts and inline errors: a status icon, a semibold title, a line of detail, and a dismiss button. It sits on the raised surface with the Float shadow.
- **Error card:** a Notice with a danger-soft fill, a 40% danger line, no shadow, and its actions under the text.

### Inputs / Fields
- **Style:** 1px `border` line on the deep background, 10px corners, 40px high.
- **Focus:** the border turns accent, with the global 2px accent outline at no offset.
- **Error / Disabled:** the border turns danger with a message below; disabled drops to 50% opacity.
- **Field:** a 13px label above, and a hint or error below at 13px.
- **Switch and Segmented:** the switch fills with accent when on. The selected segment uses `surface-hover`, not accent, because a setting is not an action.

### Navigation
- **Sidebar:** the founder's chosen Wensity sidebar, kept close to the original. It sits on `background` with a seam on its right. Rows are 13.5px medium text with 10px corners; hover takes a 4% foreground wash. The active row carries a neutral 6% pill that glides from the previous item. Group labels are 10.5px uppercase with 0.08em tracking, the one place uppercase labels are allowed, because the founder chose them. At the top: New chat, Search, Saved answers and Settings, with shortcut hints as small count badges. At the bottom, where the original shows the signed-in user: the local profile, since there is no account. Folders and archives are nested groups that unfold. The scrollbar only shows while the list scrolls.
- **Rail:** collapsed, the sidebar is a 60px strip of the same four icons and the profile mark. The logo expands it.
- **Width and size:** on a computer, its edge drags between 200 and 480px (arrow keys work on the handle, double-click resets), and the width is remembered. Settings › Appearance offers three row sizes: compact (12.5px text), default (13.5px) and large (15px).
- **Long histories:** the dated chat list is virtualised, so only the rows on screen exist.
- **Empty chat:** a greeting that follows the hour, a 44px display headline, a line naming the model and the host it will reach, and four suggestion cards chosen for the time of day. They enter in 260ms with a 40ms stagger.
- **Chat icons:** each chat row carries an icon guessed from its title (code, maths, travel, writing…), a bubble otherwise.
- **Model avatars:** a 20px circle with the model's initials, in one of five hues that avoid blue, above each answer.
- **Stream line:** a 2px accent line under the header creeps forward as text arrives, then completes and fades.
- **Costs:** under a cent they are faint, above the user's threshold they turn amber.
- **Pinned bar:** pinned messages sit as chips under the header; reaching one flashes it once.
- **Launch mark:** the first time the app opens, the N draws itself stroke by stroke over a second, once, never under reduced motion.
- **Settings tabs:** vertical with icons, and a 2px accent line that travels to the active tab. Horizontal on phones.
- **Mobile:** a drawer with scrim that follows the finger, and every row grows to a 44px target.
- **Landing header:** the wordmark, then text links in muted that turn foreground on hover.

### Popovers, Dialogs and Sheets
- **Floating panel:** raised surface, seam, Float shadow, a 150ms scale-and-fade from the trigger's corner.
- **Dialog:** built on native `<dialog>`, with 16px corners, a 20px inset, and a seam above its footer.
- **Phone:** panels become draggable bottom sheets with a small handle.

### The Composer
The floating centre of the app, and a morphing pill. Closed, it is a 48px pill on `surface`, 420px wide, showing only the placeholder and the action button. It springs open to the reading width on focus, or as soon as you type anywhere, and stays open while it holds text, files or a running response. Its border turns accent while open. Model, response style and attach sit in one row under the text. The round action button changes shape: send when there is something to send, microphone when the browser can dictate, stop during generation or dictation. Files slide out on a tab from behind the card.

### The Wire (signature)
The product's signature, and the memorable moment of the whole system. Under the composer, a stem drops from the panel to a 6px accent terminal (the stem only shows while the composer is open), then a 1px line at 40% accent runs to the host name in mono. It is a real link to the privacy settings, and it is true: the host is the host the message will reach.

- **At rest:** the line is still. The words "direct to" precede the host, and hide on phones so the host always fits.
- **Generating:** a quarter-length pulse of solid accent travels the line every 1.1s. Under reduced motion the pulse holds still.
- **Relay on:** a hollow amber terminal and the word "relay" are drawn on the line. Shape and word carry this state, since amber sits close to gold.
- **Retrying or rate limited:** the terminal, the line and the pulse turn amber, and the label says "retrying" or "rate limited by".
- **Broken:** when the last answer failed because of the route (key, network, CORS, server), or the provider's last test failed, the line is drawn broken: two dashed red ends with a × between them, and "failed:" before the host. It links to the providers page. Colour never carries this alone: the shape and the word do too.
- **First connection:** the first time a provider connects, the line draws itself from the terminal to the host in 640ms. It happens once per provider.
- **Answer finished:** the line glows once and settles, over 700ms. Both moments are skipped under reduced motion.
- **On the landing page:** the same line runs from "Your browser" to a hollow foreground terminal at a calmer 2.4s pulse, with the host changing in step with the preview. Section stations reuse the hollow accent terminal on a vertical rail.

**The True Wire Rule.** The wire only ever shows the real route. Nothing is drawn on it unless it is really on the path, and it never becomes decoration detached from a route.

### Motion
Easing is one strong ease-out for state and entrances; drawers and sheets keep the motion of their drawer library. Page load gets a single orchestrated rise (620ms, staggered by 70ms). The stream cursor is a 2px accent bar. Reduced motion collapses all of it.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** take every colour from `src/styles/tokens.css`, so a surface follows all five palettes and both themes.
- **Do** define a new palette as a hue, a tint and two accents, and add its hex to `src/lib/palettes.ts`.
- **Do** keep the accent for action, focus, selection, live state and the wire.
- **Do** separate regions in the page plane with 1px seams and small steps of lightness.
- **Do** keep destructive actions outlined and apart until hover or keyboard focus.
- **Do** show the real host on the wire, and draw the relay on it when the relay is on.
- **Do** set hosts, URLs, keys and code in mono with ligatures off, and numbers in tabular figures.
- **Do** bring every control to 44px below 768px, and turn floating panels into bottom sheets.
- **Do** label anything illustrative with a plain caption such as "Example conversation."
- **Do** gate hover styles behind `(hover: hover)` and respect reduced motion.

### Don't:
- **Don't** use blue as a palette hue, neutral tint or accent.
- **Don't** let the interface drift into a neutral grey vendor chat clone; neutrals always carry the palette hue, Mono being the one deliberate exception.
- **Don't** use a glowing AI gradient, or a gradient on any surface, text or accent. The neutral skeleton shimmer is the only gradient in the system.
- **Don't** put a shadow on resting content in the page plane, or add a second shadow style.
- **Don't** add uppercase tracked labels above headings. Headings stand alone, and wide tracking belongs to the wordmark. The sidebar's group labels are the single exception.
- **Don't** put a robot, brain or generic AI circle in the logo or any brand mark.
- **Don't** add a stop on the wire that is not on the real route.
- **Don't** fill a destructive button at rest.
- **Don't** add a radius outside 6, 10 and 16px, apart from concentric inner corners and full rounds.
